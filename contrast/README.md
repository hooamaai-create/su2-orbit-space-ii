# Where 2D Yang–Mills stops being solvable — measured, not asserted

**Scope, stated first.** This is not an attack on the Clay mass-gap problem and
proves nothing about it. It runs one SU(2) lattice engine in 2D and in 4D,
measures the same observables in both, and checks each against the one
reasoning that solves 2D exactly — so the place where that reasoning breaks in
4D can be read off data. Every number below is regenerated by `report.py` from
`results.json` into `REPORT.txt`.

## The 2D argument, in one paragraph

In 2D a gluon has D − 2 = 0 polarisations: there is nothing that propagates. In
axial gauge the lattice plaquettes become **independent variables**, the path
integral factorises into one-plaquette integrals, and each is a known Bessel
function. Everything follows exactly: `⟨P⟩ = u(β) = I₂(β)/I₁(β)`,
`W(R,T) = u^(R·T)` (a pure area law, so exact confinement), and no correlation
between distinct plaquettes (no particles). The "gap" is the energy of a flux
line, `σ = −ln u`, not a glueball mass.

In 4D the same one-plaquette answer is only the **leading term of the
strong-coupling expansion** — valid at small β (coarse lattice), where it gives
a *provable* area law and gap (Osterwalder–Seiler), and increasingly wrong as β
grows toward the continuum. The question is where.

## The engine is right

- Gauge invariance: a random gauge transformation changes the plaquette by
  6·10⁻¹⁸ and Wilson loops by 2·10⁻¹⁷.
- 2D plaquette against the exact answer at β = 1, 2, 4, 8:
  0.2396(4) / 0.2402, 0.4334(4) / 0.4331, 0.6584(3) / 0.6580,
  0.8193(1) / 0.8192.
- 2D Wilson loops: every resolvable R×T loop up to 4×4 matches `u^(R·T)`, max
  pull 2.2σ across 49 loops. At β = 8 the Creutz ratios are 0.199, 0.203, 0.210
  against −ln u = 0.199 — flat in R, the pure area law.
- 2D neighbouring-slice correlation C(1)/C(0): +0.002(5), −0.002(5),
  −0.003(6), +0.004(6) — zero at every β. No state propagates.

That is 2D Yang–Mills solved, reproduced numerically.

## What happens in 4D

| β | ⟨P⟩ vs u | Creutz χ(2) vs −ln u | W(3,3) vs u⁹ | C(1)/C(0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | +1.3% | 1.295(64) vs 1.426 | — (below noise) | 0.001(14) |
| 1.5 | +5.3% | 0.994(15) vs 1.067 | — (below noise) | −0.005(16) |
| 2.0 | +15.7% | 0.600(4) vs 0.837 | 7× | **0.074(12)** |
| 2.2 | +22.6% | 0.406(2) vs 0.767 | 17× | **0.142(13)** |
| 2.4 | +27.7% | 0.252(1) vs 0.706 | 34× | **0.084(16)** |
| 2.6 | +28.8% | 0.187(1) vs 0.653 | 38× | **0.074(14)** |

Four things break, and they break together:

1. **Factorisation.** At β = 1.0 the plaquette is within 1.3% of the
   one-plaquette answer — 2D-style reasoning nearly works. By β = 2.4 it is 28%
   off, and a 3×3 Wilson loop is 34× larger than independent plaquettes allow
   (pulls of hundreds of σ). The plaquettes are no longer independent, because
   in 4D each link belongs to six plaquettes and the Bianchi identity ties them.
2. **The pure area law.** In 2D the Creutz ratio is the same at every R. In 4D
   at β = 2.4 it falls 0.252 → 0.145 → 0.076 with R: short loops feel
   gluon exchange (the Coulomb part of the potential), which 2D doesn't have.
   Only large loops see the string tension.
3. **The strong-coupling string tension.** At β = 2.4 the largest-loop Creutz
   ratio, χ(4) = 0.076(19), is ~9× smaller than −ln u = 0.706. The physical scale is no longer set by the
   coupling directly — it is being generated dynamically (asymptotic freedom),
   and falls much faster with β than any finite order of strong coupling
   tracks.
4. **Particles.** In 2D neighbouring time slices are uncorrelated at every β.
   In 4D from β = 2.0 they are correlated at 5–11σ: a state propagates. That
   state is the glueball — the thing whose mass *is* the Clay problem's gap.

The crossover — between β ≈ 1.5 and 2.2 — is where every 2D-style argument dies
at once. It is also exactly where the rigorous strong-coupling proof of the gap
stops: proving the gap in the continuum would mean carrying control through
this region and beyond it, to β → ∞, and nobody knows how.

## What this does not show

- **It does not measure the glueball mass.** The effective mass from
  unsmeared plaquettes between t = 0 and 1 is 2.0–2.6 in lattice units at
  β ≥ 2.0. Published smeared-operator studies put the 0⁺⁺ mass at roughly 1 at
  β = 2.4. Unsmeared operators at the first time step are dominated by heavier
  states, so these are upper bounds, not a gap measurement. Getting the real
  value needs smearing, a variational basis, and larger lattices.
- At β ≤ 1.5 the 4D slice correlator is also compatible with zero — not
  because there's no glueball, but because strong coupling puts its mass at
  4–6 in lattice units, so C(1)/C(0) ≈ e^(−m) is below this run's noise.
  The 2D/4D contrast in the correlator is only resolved at β ≥ 2.0.
- L = 8 in 4D; R = 4 loops span half the lattice. The largest Creutz ratios
  carry finite-size effects.
- No continuum extrapolation. Nothing here bears on existence of the continuum
  theory or on the Clay problem.

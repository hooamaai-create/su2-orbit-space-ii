# A decay flow for the number of dimensions — reconstruction and audit

**This is not part of the SU(2) record.** It is a separate, far weaker piece of
work that shares the repository only because it shares the method: register the
test, run it, and report what it kills. Nothing here is lattice gauge theory,
nothing here is measured against data, and nothing here should be cited
alongside the results in the top-level README.

## What was claimed

A toy universe in which dimension is not a parameter but a statistic of
correlations, evolved under stability rules taken from textbook mechanics:
structure cannot hold together above the bound-state threshold, and the
interaction that erases it stops propagating below the gravitational one. Run
from an effective dimension of 6.10, it decayed over 112 steps and froze at
2.97, where the instability rate switched itself off — with the claim that
**nothing in the code said "go to 3."**

## What this directory does

`dim_flow.py` is an independent re-implementation from that description: a
cloud of N events whose dimension is *measured* every step by a fixed-radius
maximum-likelihood estimator (never stored), a rate built from the two
thresholds, and a move that erases directional support. `dim_sweep.py` varies
one thing at a time and asks whether the halting dimension follows it.
`SPEC.md` registers the predictions and the reading of each outcome; it was
written after the single default run and **before** the sweep output was read.
One thing in it was amended after data — the scoring statistic — and because
that amendment is what flipped the headline verdict, it is written up as
Amendment 1 at the top of `SPEC.md` rather than buried. `regen.py` regenerates
every number quoted below from the raw JSON into `TALLIES.txt`.

## What reproduced

It reproduced, and closely. An independent implementation of the mechanism, run
from an initial measured dimension of **6.121**, decayed to **3.066** in **179
steps** and stopped there because the instability rate had fallen by a factor
of **1619** and crossed the stopping tolerance — `rate_vanished`, not a step
budget running out. The exponential-approach fit puts the asymptote at
**3.011**.

The step count is not comparable across implementations (different move,
different step scale), but the shape is: a fast fall, a long slow approach, and
a rate that shuts itself off rather than being switched off. Two details are
worth keeping:

- The flow **does not stall at 4**. Starting from 4.6, 6.0, 8.3 and 9.9 it lands
  at 3.035–3.111 every time, passing straight through the bound-state
  threshold. That is real dynamical content, and it comes from the ensemble's
  *dispersion*: with local dimensions spread around the mean, some fraction is
  super-critical for any mean below 4, so the decay never switches itself off
  at the upper threshold. Nothing puts that in by hand.
- The reported halt at **2.97 sits just below 3**, where this implementation
  halts on average **+0.054 above** the floor. A halt below the floor means
  either estimator noise or a finite step overshooting a zero it approaches
  asymptotically — worth checking in the original, since it is the signature of
  the halting point being discretisation-limited rather than rate-limited.

## What the audit found

All numbers below are regenerated by `regen.py`; the instrument calibration
(raw = 0.8948 x known, max residual 0.044 over d = 1..8) is applied to every
dimension quoted here, and F-D3 did not fire.

| registered | test | result | |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | flow self-arrests | 5/5 seeds halt on `rate_vanished` | **PASS** |
| D2 | seed stability | spread 0.041 (limit 0.15) | **PASS** |
| D3 | the one soft parameter, w = 0.25..1.00 | spread 0.038 (limit 0.15) | **PASS** |
| D4 | step size, eta0 = 0.3..1.2 | spread 0.027 (limit 0.15) | **PASS** |
| D5 | basin from d0 = 4.6..9.9 | 3.035..3.111, spread 0.077; none stall at 4 | **PASS** |
| D6 | upper threshold, d_collapse = 3.5..5.0 | spread 0.049 (limit 0.20) | **PASS** |
| D7 | **move the floor and see if the halt follows** | **slope 0.947, R² 0.9997** | **FIRES** |

D2–D6 are the good news, and they are not nothing: the halting dimension does
not ride on the soft parameter, the step size, the ensemble size, the seed, or
the threshold that starts the decay. Whatever sets it is not a knob.

D7 says what does set it. Handing the flow a different gravitational floor
moves the halt with it, one for one:

```
floor placed at 2.0  ->  halted at 2.103
floor placed at 2.5  ->  halted at 2.551
floor placed at 3.0  ->  halted at 3.043
floor placed at 3.5  ->  halted at 3.518
```

slope 0.947, R² 0.9997, mean offset +0.054 above the floor. (A fifth run with
the floor at 1.5 was still falling after 2500 steps and is excluded as
unconverged — the decay slows as the super-critical fraction empties out.)

**So the loop did not choose three. It found the zero it was handed, and found
it robustly.** The branch reading that makes slope > 0.8 mean this was fixed in
`SPEC.md` before the sweep ran, and it retires the "nothing in the code said go
to 3" language. The scoring statistic underneath it was not: it was repaired
after the first sweep, and on the broken version D7 read the other way. That is
Amendment 1, and it is the one place in this directory where a post-hoc choice
changed a verdict — in the direction of the claim failing rather than passing. What
survives is a mechanism — a decay that passes through the bound-state
threshold under its own dispersion and self-arrests where the eraser stops
propagating — and the number 3 is inherited from linearised GR's graviton
count, not produced by the dynamics.

One more registered outcome, confirmed: started **below** the floor at 2.623,
the flow halts after a single step and stays there. There is no restoring term,
so this is an absorbing halt, not a two-sided attractor. "Fixed point" is the
wrong word for it; "the place the decay stops" is the right one.

## The convention problem — which is the real finding

Both thresholds are real physics, but they are not stated in the same units,
and under any single consistent convention the pair does not bracket our world.

Take **d spatial**. Circular orbits in a `-k/r^(d-2)` potential are stable iff
d < 4, and the Coulomb problem falls to the centre for d >= 4 — so the upper
threshold is 4. The graviton polarisation count in D spacetime dimensions is
`D(D-3)/2`; with D = d+1 that is `(d+1)(d-2)/2`, which vanishes at **d = 2**,
not 3. Thresholds (4, 2); a continuous flow lands on 2 spatial dimensions.

Take **d spacetime**. Orbits live in D-1 spatial dimensions, so stability needs
D < 5. The graviton count vanishes at D = 3. Thresholds (5, 3); the flow lands
on D = 3, which is again **two** spatial dimensions.

Either way the halting point is a universe with **two** spatial dimensions,
where gravity carries no propagating degrees of freedom and has no Newtonian
inverse-square limit. The pairing that reproduces our world, upper 4 with lower 3,
takes the upper threshold in spatial units and the lower one in spacetime
units. That mixing, not the loop, is what puts the answer at 3.

There *is* an argument that singles out three **spatial** dimensions from
below, and it is Ehrenfest's own: sharp wave propagation — Huygens' principle,
signals without tails — holds only in odd spatial dimensions, so 3 is the
smallest that carries clean radiation. But it is a **parity** condition. It
takes no value at non-integer d and cannot be the zero of a continuous rate, so
this class of mechanism cannot reach it. A flow that halts because a
coefficient goes to zero can only halt where some continuous quantity vanishes,
and no continuous quantity vanishes at three spatial dimensions.

## The CDT-facing prediction, and what it is worth

The frozen state's spectral dimension — computed as CDT computes it, from the
return probability of a diffusion, `d_s(tau) = -2 dlogP/dlogtau`, at N = 30000
with an exact d = 3 cloud carried alongside as a control:

| diffusion time | frozen state | control (real d = 3) |
|---|---|---|
| 4–8 (UV) | 5.027 | 2.429 |
| 8–16 | 3.634 | 2.641 |
| 16–32 | 2.346 | 2.916 |
| 32–64 | 1.877 | 2.929 |
| 64–128 | 1.657 | 2.736 |
| 128–256 (IR) | 1.294 | 2.404 |

The frozen state runs **5.03 down to 1.29**; the control stays within 0.6 of 3
across the same range and drifts in neither direction overall, so the trend is
not the instrument. CDT runs the other
way: ~4 in the infrared, ~2 in the ultraviolet. The two really do disagree in
sign.

The honest weight to put on that: **the sign is structural, not predicted.**
This model reduces dimension by making directions thin. A direction that is
thin is invisible to a large measuring ball and visible to a small one, so any
model in this class must give more dimensions in the UV than in the IR. The
disagreement with CDT is a disagreement between two mechanism classes —
dimensional reduction by hiding directions, versus dimensional reduction by
short-distance quantum geometry — and it was fixed the moment the mechanism was
chosen, not derived from it afterwards. It is still falsifiable, and it is
still worth stating; it is not evidence.

A sharper problem sits underneath. The halting value is the dimension measured
**in one window** — the ball the dynamics happens to use. Sweep the ball across
the frozen state and it reads 10.37, 8.37, 6.08, 4.13, 3.07, 2.24: a range of
**8.12**. The same sweep on a real 3-dimensional space moves by **0.70**. So
"the universe froze at 3" means "the state reads 3 at one scale", and it reads
ten at another. That is a much weaker sentence than it sounds, and it must not
be shortened to the stronger one.

## What would turn this into a result

One change, and it is specific. Replace the halt-at-a-zero with a **balance**:
a decay term from super-critical instability, as now, plus a growth term whose
strength is the gravitational binding available at dimension d, which weakens
as d falls. The fixed point is then wherever the two rates cross — a location
that is *computed* from the two physical inputs rather than inserted as the
zero of one of them, and a **two-sided** attractor, which this model's is not.
That version has a real test the present one cannot pass: start below the fixed
point and watch the dimension rise. Until something rises, "fixed point" means
"the place the decay stops", and a decay that stops where its coefficient
vanishes has told us about the coefficient.

The same test applies to the original code and takes five minutes there: move
the gravitational threshold off 3 and see whether the halt moves with it. The
D7 row above is that test run here.

## Files

```
python dimension/dim_flow.py --out dimension/results/flow_default.json
PYTHONPATH=dimension python dimension/dim_sweep.py --out dimension/results/sweep.json
python dimension/regen.py > dimension/TALLIES.txt
```
